## 3. Refits

2034 refits; 2006 reported convergence (28 did not; kept and counted, never dropped). R2 swap flags (|φ₁ − φ₀| < 0.05): 53. Degenerate refits (a regime with ergodic mass < 10 % or expected duration < 2 sessions: an outlier component, not a regime) by series: φ-switching AR(1), φ 0.3 / 0.9 (63) 5%; φ-switching AR(1), φ 0.3 / 0.9 (252) 18%; constant-φ 0.6 AR(1) null 78%; σ-switching noise, σ 1 / 2 (63) 0%; σ-switching noise, σ 1 / 2 (252) 3%; fGn H 0.5 null 86%.

## 4. Compute

Ordinary session: Hamilton update 0.040 ms, BOCPD step over three hazards 0.646 ms. Refit session (1 in 21): one statsmodels fit on 1008 sessions plus a 1029-row filter, 204–257 ms. Target < 50 ms per decision: met on ordinary sessions, not on refit sessions (the refit can run after the close, before the next decision).

## 6. Reading (hand-written after the run; not regenerated by the script)

- **R2 (MS-AR(1)) is the one model that adds something over its baseline, on strong synthetic regimes.**
  - With φ alternating 0.3 / 0.9, R2 reaches Youden J 0.63 (mean duration 63) and 0.67 (252), against the rolling-φ
    baseline B4's 0.30 and 0.53.
  - Its Brier score is about half B4's, and its median delay is 4 / 10 sessions.
  - The caveat that matters for the market:
    - On a constant-φ series (no regimes) R2 still calls "fast reversion" 40 % of the time, and 78 % of its refits
      there are degenerate.
    - On random-walk d*_t its two φs sit near the random-walk 0.71, often closer together than the 0.05 swap
      threshold.
    - So an R2 signal on market data means something only if the fitted φs are well apart and the refits are not
      degenerate. U10 must report both alongside any R2 gate.
- **R1 (HMM on standardized returns) does not beat trailing volatility.**
  - Youden J is 0.58 vs B1's 0.58 at duration 63, and 0.42 vs 0.51 at 252. R1 reacts faster (delay 3 vs 6–10)
    but has more false positives.
  - On fGn it signals "high σ" 56 % of the time, and 86 % of those refits are degenerate. With no regime the
    σ-ordering is arbitrary, as the review predicted.
  - Standardizing by the trailing-252 σ (§6.4) removes much of the regime signal the HMM is meant to find. A slow
    σ regime shows up in z only for about a year after it starts.
- **R3 (BOCPD) is conservative.** It catches a σ doubling within 21 sessions in only 21 % of switches (23 of 109 at
  duration 63), but almost never raises a false alarm (0.1–0.7 per 1,000). As §6.4's stability gate ("no recent
  change"), it will be on nearly always, so it can barely condition anything.
- **Compute.**
  - Ordinary decisions take well under 1 ms, against the < 50 ms target.
  - A refit takes 0.2–0.26 s, once every 21 sessions. On those sessions a decision exceeds 50 ms, but the refit can
    run after the close and before the next decision.
  - 28 of 2,034 refits did not report convergence. They were kept and counted.
- **What this says about improving on the vanilla rule.**
  - These are synthetic regimes, and strong ones: φ 0.3 vs 0.9, or σ doubling.
  - R2 is the only candidate that clearly separates them better than a rolling statistic. It is also the model most
    prone to inventing regimes where there are none.
  - Whether d*_t has reversion-speed regimes of that size is a market question. Under the routing it is answered by
    H3 on `diag` (B1 only) unless the user amends the routing, and by H4's R2-weighted blend (U11).
